Welcome to Tapestry Con

What does it mean to travel toward ourselves and one another till we reach the light?

Tapestry Con is our response to the deafening noise created by singular narratives of ways of being, meaning, and dignity, enforced through political, economic and embodied violence. As Africans, we often cannot hear ourselves think over this noise, which has real consequences for those of us living in a world sustained by continued subjugation and exploitation.

Our doors are open, come in  

Location: Madhouse, University of Lagos
Date: October 16th to October 18th, 2026.

Across three days, We will stand alongside you and weave together diverse threads of knowledge, experience, creativity and aesthetics across disciplines and geographies. This is not a space for uplifting and consolidating a single answer, it is a sacred attempt at creating the conditions for new dreams to come alive through the richness of our interconnected, yet distinct, realities and aesthetics. Tapestry Con 2026 will bring together thinkers, artists, researchers, practitioners, students and members of the wider community for conversations, performances, workshops, exhibitions, shared meals and embodied experiences.

CALL FOR SUBMISSIONS | TAPESTRY CON 2026

SESSION TITLE: TAKE WHAT YOU HAVE LEFT AND HEAL YOURSELF: A MULTIMODAL EXPLORATORY SESSION

Themes: Confronting Colonial Exploitation and Subjugation; Creating New, Dignified Realities for African Peoples.

Sub-themes: the neocolonial state, knowledge, culture, economies of care, knowledge traditions, land, ancestry, sovereignty, art as memory work, rest, the erotic, militarisation, economic and social violence, afrofuturism, dreams of new worlds.

We are calling for submissions for “Take What You Have Left and Heal Yourself”, a multidisciplinary, multimodal session bringing together artists, writers, thinkers, and practitioners across different forms and disciplines. Through performance, visual art, spoken word, video, abstract presentations, and cultural practice, we will explore what collective flourishing looks like, Africa's political economy, and how power interacts with resources to create the material conditions of our lives under colonial logics.
